# Retro

**Goal:** **action fields** — areas worth working on, each with an
understanding of what produces it. Not complaints, not solutions. A solution that
comes up gets noted and parked for co-design.

## Before you open

Read the profile, the last retro, and the open action fields with their solution
approaches. Sweep for structure — rhythm, repetition, stalling, where they are the
bottleneck, drift from their goals, what changed. **Count and compare**: "fourteen
a week since June, up from eight" is a finding; "a lot of meetings" is not. One
instance is an anecdote; three is a pattern. Hold all of it until step 4.

## Flow

1. **Set the stage:** figure out the scope with the user (general or one area), period, and one line on how it
   has felt. Mention open action fields; you come back to them at the end.
2. **What is going well** — first, and meant. For each: why did it work?
3. **What is grinding** — collect several before going deep on one. Notice what
   they skip or joke about: "you've mentioned X twice now, in passing."
4. **Put your findings on the table** — two or three, the structural one first,
   each with its source, ending on a question. They may wave it away.
   > "You've been in about fourteen meetings a week since June, up from eight, and
   > nine are recurring ones you don't own. Real change, or just this time of
   > year?"
5. **Go beneath the symptoms** — most of the session. Ask why, three or four
   honest times. Separate symptom, cause and constraint out loud. Look for one
   mechanism behind several complaints. Ask what would have to be true for it to
   stop.
6. **Converge on action fields**, fill them from the template, read them back:
   "Are these the right things to work on — anything missing or not worth it?"
   Save them as soon as they say yes. A field continuing an existing one keeps its
   name and is updated, not started over. Then close the loop on earlier open fields
   and approaches: still live, changed, holding, done?
7. **Write the retro.**
8. **Offer co-design:** "Which of these is bothering you most?" Yes → `co-design`.

## Pitfalls

- **Conclusions instead of events** ("we need better data quality", "I should
  finally do X"). Don't accept it as the cause, don't ask for the incidents again.
  Run a retro of the attempt: What have you tried? What stopped it? What would
  have to be different this time? **What does not having it cost you right now?**
  Record the mechanism one level below what they offered, never the plan they
  walked in with.
- **Cause unclear** — write that it is unclear. An honest gap beats a tidy guess.
