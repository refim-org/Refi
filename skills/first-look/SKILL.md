# First look

**Goal:** something useful today. Read their recent work, show them what you see,
fix one small thing. It sees the shape of a problem from the outside, not the
mechanism underneath — say so once, at the end.

## 1. Find

If the sweep already ran in this conversation — at the first meeting, or before a
get-to-know — build on it and only look further where something needs checking.
Otherwise look at the last four to six weeks, source by source, several searches
each. You are looking for **patterns, not incidents**. Examples:

| Pattern | Where you see it | A finding when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No room to think** | Calendar: back-to-back days, no free block of 90+ minutes | Most days of most weeks |
| **Meetings they don't own** | Calendar: recurring series they only attend, no agenda, many attendees | Several hours a week, or growing |
| **Work spilling over** | Calendar, mail, chat: activity early, late, at weekends | A regular rhythm, not one crunch |
| **The same thing again** | Mail, chat, tickets: the same question, request or correction from the same people | Three times or more |
| **Waiting on them** | Unanswered threads, reviews and approvals in their name, tickets assigned and untouched | Others are blocked by it |
| **Stuck elsewhere** | Threads where they sent the last message, tickets not moving, decisions postponed | Weeks without movement |
| **Starting from zero** | Documents: similar things written from scratch — proposals, reports, agendas | Several in the period |
| **Rework** | Tickets reopened, documents with many revision rounds, "as discussed" corrections | Recurs on similar work |
| **Drift** | Where the time goes vs. their goals and profile, if there is one | Clear gap |

And look for **what goes well**: where they unblock others, what gets thanked,
what ships reliably. You will name two or three of those too.

Make every finding checkable:

- **Count it** — "fourteen meetings a week", not "a lot of meetings".
- **Compare it** — to an earlier stretch ("up from eight in spring"), or to what
  they said matters.
- **Three instances make a pattern**; one is an anecdote.
- **Name the source** and keep observed ("your calendar shows") apart from
  inferred ("that probably means").
- **Skip what is plainly personal.**

Then rank: how often it happens × what it costs them × how much of it is in their
own hands. Lead with the highest, not the most dramatic.

With no access to their work, there is nothing to read from the outside. Offer a get-to-know.

## 2. Show

**Four or five observations in one message** — the one place a long turn is right.
Each with its source and its number. Include the two or three strengths, with the
same sourcing. **Observations only, no advice yet.** Then ask what they make of
it, and where you got it wrong.

## 3. Name the themes

Cluster into **two to four themes** and save each as an action field,
`status: draft`. The cause is your guess — write it as one: "Likely … — inferred
from …, not yet checked."

> "Which of these is actually costing you? And tell me if I've got any wrong."

A rejected theme becomes `dropped`. If nothing lands, say so and offer a
get-to-know.

## 4. Fix one

For the theme they pick, check it is a **quick win**: inside their own control,
set up in minutes, mostly doable by you. If it depends on other people changing,
or needs real building, say so — that is a `co-design` or `retro`, and you hand
over.

Typical quick wins, and the part you potentially can do yourself:

| Pattern | Quick win | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No room to think | Recurring focus blocks at their best hours | Create the series |
| Meetings they don't own | Decline, shorten, or ask for an agenda or async update | Draft the message |
| The same thing again | One reusable answer, FAQ or snippet | Write it from their best past reply |
| Waiting on them | A fixed triage slot, plus a list of what waits now | Create the slot, build the list |
| Stuck elsewhere | Follow-ups for the oldest stuck threads | Draft them |
| Starting from zero | A template from their best past example | Build it |
| Rework | A short checklist for the step that keeps bouncing | Write it |
| Work spilling over | A wind-down block and a hard stop | Create it |
| Mail noise | A filter or rule | Propose it exactly, create it if you can |

Ask first what they have already tried — it is the question that stops you
rebuilding what they abandoned last year. Then agree it in three lines: what
changes, when it happens in their week, how you both will know it works. Build it
— **say, yes, do, report**. Save it as a solution approach, and the field moves to
`agreed`.

## 5. Close

Once: "We got here from the outside, and never got to why this happens. If it
comes back, a proper retro is worth more than another fix." If there is no
profile yet, offer the get-to-know as the natural next step — it starts from what
you have just seen together.
